## Q&A prep (English answers — memorize the beats, not the words)

### Q: "Signature Regalia is premium gear — does it have stats? Isn't that pay-to-win?"
**Honest answer:**
> "Yes. Signature gear carries stats and paid items can meaningfully strengthen a build. That is intentional for our RO/SEA audience, but every purchase is direct: players see the item, its effect, and its price before they buy. We do not sell random rolls or loot boxes. Players can also farm and trade toward premium gear through the Steam Market; that path becomes stronger as the player economy gains liquidity."

**Investor room:**
> "Power is a primary revenue driver, not a cosmetic disguise. Tradeability gives spenders a route to resell while giving grinders a route toward premium gear, which supports liquidity and creates a secondary-market fee stream. The design goal is pay-to-progress with clear products and a functioning player economy, rather than opaque RNG monetization."

- Do not claim that paid power is cosmetic, that it cannot win, or that every player can obtain it easily for free at launch.
- Caveat: the free-to-trade path depends on market liquidity; say *"it strengthens as the economy grows,"* not *"free players get it easily on day one."*

### Q: "With no marketing budget, how do you get your first players?"
> "The grant funds the product, not marketing — that's deliberate. In six months it takes us to a launch-ready core with a live Steam page gathering wishlists — the top of the funnel, built within the grant. Growth comes after, and we fund it ourselves, like we self-fund engineering. Our channel is genre creators: the Ragnarok and idle-game audiences already exist, and the Desktop Companion gives them something new to show, so it earns coverage. Free-to-play means their viewers install in one click. And Steam compounds it — wishlists drive launch visibility, and idle games rack up high concurrent players, pushing us onto the 'Most Played' list. The grant gets us to the starting line; growth is our job from there."

**RO-community pull (spoken):** *"Ragnarok Online is hugely popular across Thailand and Southeast Asia — that audience already exists, and they're our most natural channel. And there's a second, powerful pull: this is an idle game where the gear you farm has real, tradeable value on the Steam Market — so the time you put in actually rewards you. A familiar Ragnarok-style world plus rewards you can trade is a strong hook for exactly these players."* (Investor room: can say "earn real value back" directly; depa/policy room: keep "tradeable value" to stay clear of RMT/gambling framing.)

**GTM channel — tiered by what's actually reachable on an indie budget:**
- **Primary channel (within our budget):** **small/mid idle- & Ragnarok-genre influencers, ~10k–100k followers.** We've researched the niche — there are *many* of them internationally, and creators this size are **affordable within our (self-funded) marketing budget**. The game is free-to-play, so there's no key to hand out — they just download and try it. Realistic, sized, low-cost.
- **Thai — the sweet spot** (local, low-cost, real relationships): *Apologize Ch* (ApologizeZ — RO/MMORPG niche, exact audience) · *JayMeander* (RO Origin). A *genuine feature* from *9arm* (~1.55M, veteran RO player + tech audience that loves the engine/fair-online angle) is plausible-but-earned, not a paid deal.
- **Organic upside only (can't buy, can hope):** if the Desktop Companion catches on, big idle creators (*DangerouslyFunny* ~2.76M, *The Spiffing Brit*) pick it up for free because it's novel. **Not a line item** — million-sub sponsorships ($1k–10k+/video) are unrealistic on a ฿200K product grant.
- **Paid influencer** scales in later, revenue-funded — not the grant.
- **Spoken line:** *"There are many small idle- and Ragnarok-genre creators in the ten-to-a-hundred-thousand-follower range — we've researched them, and creators that size are within our budget. The game is free-to-play, so their viewers just download and try it. That's our early channel; big creators are upside if it catches, not the plan."*

**Other channels — organic / free (reinforces "no big marketing budget needed"):**
- **Steam Next Fest** — Valve's free demo event with a built-in visibility + wishlist spike; a playable demo during the Fest is one of the strongest wishlist drivers for an indie Steam launch, and it fits our 6-month "Steam page + gather wishlists" milestone. (Needs a demo build — same asset as the live pitch demo.)
- **Reddit — our exact niche lives there:** *r/incremental_games* (the idle/incremental community — indie idle devs launch here organically), plus *r/RagnarokOnline*, *r/pcgaming*, *r/IndieGaming*. Free, high-intent, EN reach.
- **Thai Facebook pages & groups** — Facebook is the dominant gaming-discovery platform in Thailand; RO/MMO and indie-game pages are low-cost, high-reach, and a natural home-turf advantage for a Thai dev.
- **Spoken line (channels):** *"Discovery doesn't need a big budget: a demo at Steam Next Fest for a wishlist spike, the idle-game community on Reddit where our exact players are, and Thailand's gaming Facebook pages where we have home-turf reach."*

### Q: "The revenue is higher than your submitted document — did you inflate it?"
> "Since submission we added a new premium layer — seasonal collectibles plus a ten-percent marketplace fee. Our player assumptions are **unchanged** from the submitted document; every baht of the increase comes from these new streams, not from assuming more players."

### Q: "What assumptions does the collectibles number use?" (two layers)
1. **Attach rate + price, benchmarked to real Steam economy games** — CS2 capsules, PoE supporter packs, Dota treasures. Our ARPPU blend = 70% pass-only (~฿1,500/yr) + 25% (~฿4,200) + 5% collectors (~฿10,000) = **฿2,600/yr**.
2. **The floor absorbs the risk** — even if collectibles hit only HALF our assumption, the conservative floor (~฿5.2M) still stands. That's what a floor is for.

### Q: "At launch, where do the players for ฿26M come from?"
Funnel: 1k organic wishlists (6-mo) → **~30k by launch** (marketing push) → **~500k year-1 installs** (idle-CCU flywheel + creators + Steam Next Fest) → **2% pay = 10k payers × ฿2,600 = ฿26M**. Sensitivity: linear on installs — **250k = ฿13M · 500k = ฿26M · 1M = ฿52M** — the number hinges on installs (say this; own it).

### Q: "Marketing budget for launch — from where, when the grant is all art?"
> "Organic-first and low-cost — creators, Reddit, Steam Next Fest, Thai Facebook — funded modestly by us, the way we self-fund engineering. Paid user-acquisition scales only once the game earns, reinvesting revenue. We don't need a big upfront war chest — discovery is community- and platform-driven."

### Q10 — "฿200K is small — what's the real budget to ship the full game, and where does the rest come from?"
> "To reach a full launch we estimate roughly **six hundred thousand to one million baht total** — the large majority is **art**. Everything else is small: audio, Steam's fee, and infrastructure that stays cheap by design. Critically, **engineering carries no cash cost — I build the game myself.** This grant is deliberately scoped as the **first tranche** — it funds an online-ready core and a live Steam page, not the whole game. The rest comes from **self-funding**, **reinvesting early revenue** post-launch, and if we want to move faster, a **Kickstarter focused purely on art** or a follow-on round. The key point: we're **art-capacity-bound, not cash-burn-bound** — our main cost is art we commission at our own pace, not a monthly payroll. So we don't need a war chest up front, and the project doesn't die if funding comes slowly."
- One-liner: *"~฿600K–1M to full launch, mostly art; engineering is free because I build it; ops are revenue-funded after launch."*
- ⚠️ Frame the small grant as **deliberate milestone-scoping**, never "underfunded."

### Q11 — "Can players interact during combat, or is it watch-only?"
> "Right now the party **auto-battles** — that's the core idle promise; it respects your time. But it's not purely passive: on our roadmap are **player-activated abilities** you unlock and tap in during combat — saved for the moments that matter, like **bosses and special events**. So you can lean back and let it run, or lean in and play hands-on when a fight gets tough. **Idle when you want, active when you want.**"
- ⚠️ Present as **roadmap** (not yet shipped) — don't overpromise.

### Q12 — "Live-service needs ongoing maintenance — what's the monthly cost, and is that feasible for a small team?"
> "This is where our architecture pays off. The game runs a **deterministic client-sim** — each player's device runs the simulation. Our server **re-runs it only to verify the results are valid** — and it can do that on a **sample**, not live for every player every moment. That kills the biggest live-ops cost — always-on game servers. So infrastructure scales **sub-linearly**: even at hundreds of thousands of players, server cost stays a **low single-digit percent of revenue** — a few hundred dollars a month at launch. The real ongoing cost is **seasonal art**, revenue-funded and paced to the team. **No large fixed monthly burn** — which is exactly why a small team can run this sustainably."
- Numbers: launch (~tens of thousands MAU) infra **~$100–500/mo**; at scale (100k+ players) low thousands $/mo = **< 5% of revenue**; engineering self-funded, ops revenue-funded.
- Moat line: *"The server **verifies**, it doesn't **run** the game for everyone — infra is a rounding error against revenue."*

### Q13 — "Is there gacha / gambling / loot boxes?"
> "No — and that's a deliberate design choice. Every collectible is a **direct purchase of a known item at a fixed price** — you see exactly what you get *before* you buy. There's **zero randomness** anywhere in our monetization, so there's no loot box and no gacha — and randomness is what regulators define as gambling.
